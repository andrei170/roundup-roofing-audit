Your "Get A Free Quote" box is an embedded frame pulled from brownsvilleconcretecontractor.com — a live concrete business roughly 550 miles south of Dallas.

It works today. It is on all ten of your pages, and you do not control the domain it comes from. If that address is ever switched off, changed or allowed to lapse, every quote form on your site stops working and nothing on your end will tell you.

Every page of rounduproofing.com read on 21 September 2026
Finding 1 — The Exact Thing We Found
On all ten pages of rounduproofing.com, the quote form is an iframe: https://secure.brownsvilleconcretecontractor.com/widget/form/iZl3VYtxETlNVhiUEbzU That domain resolves to a live, unrelated business: "Brownsville Concrete Contractor | Concrete Services in Brownsville, TX" The widget itself is a GoHighLevel form (LeadConnector). The form loads and returns a page, so it is working right now. Pages carrying it: index, about-us, commercial-roofing, contact-us, general-construction, metal-roofing, recent-projects, roof-repair-leaks, shingle-roofing, tile-roofing
What this is, and what we are not saying

This is what a white-label setup looks like when one agency builds several sites and points them all at a single account domain. We are not saying anybody has done anything wrong, and we cannot see where your submissions actually go — that is not visible from outside.

What we can say is this: the front door to your business sits on an address registered to somebody else's company. For a business that wants five to ten more jobs a month, that is the wrong thing to be renting.

Finding 2 — Nothing On Your Site Is Measured. At All.
Tracking found across all ten pages: Google Analytics (GA4) not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Any gtag() call not found — zero across all ten pages Call tracking not found
This is the finding that costs you the most, and it is invisible

You told us the work comes from referrals and from your door-knocker, and that the D2D has been good. Here is the problem: you cannot prove any of that, and neither can you prove the opposite.

When a homeowner gets knocked on Tuesday, looks you up Thursday and calls Friday, that is a D2D lead that looks like a phone call out of nowhere. Your website is almost certainly already closing work for you and there is no way to see it. Equally, if the site is doing nothing, you are the last person who would find out.

Every competitor we checked is measuring. You are the only one flying blind.

Finding 3 — Dallas Is A Hail Market, And You Are Not Set Up To Catch A Storm
What your own site already gets right
  • You talk about storms. Storm and hail damage appear throughout the site — that is the correct message for this market.
  • Your page titles are genuinely well written. "Metal Roofing in Dallas, TX | Roundup Roofing" is exactly the right shape, on all ten pages. Most firms your size get this wrong.
  • Your phone number is tappable and your service area names Fort Worth, Plano, Irving, Garland, Frisco, McKinney, Arlington, Denton, Richardson and Mesquite.
And the part that does not work

Roofing demand in Dallas does not arrive evenly — it arrives in spikes, days after a storm. The firms that win those weeks are the ones already carrying a pixel, already retargeting, and already able to put a message in front of the neighbourhood that just got hit.

With no pixel and no analytics, you cannot advertise to anyone who has already visited your site, and you cannot target the streets that took the damage. A storm comes, your competitors switch on, and you are back to knocking doors one at a time.

Finding 4 — The Proof You Have Is Not Doing Any Work
Testimonial on the homepage yes — and it is a good one Customer name attached to it none Star rating shown "5.0 Star Rating" Number of reviews shown not stated anywhere Manufacturer certification none found (no GAF, Owens Corning or CertainTeed badge) Financing offered not mentioned Years stated "15+ Years Of Experience"
An unnamed testimonial reads as a stock quote, even when it is real

The review on your homepage sounds like a genuine customer — the detail about the crew explaining the work and cleaning up is exactly what a real person says. But with no name under it, a careful homeowner discounts it. A first name, a suburb and a date costs nothing and changes how it reads.

Every Dallas competitor we checked publishes a manufacturer certification and a financing option. Those two things are what a homeowner comparing three quotes actually looks for on a $15,000 decision.

Finding 5 — Two Of Your Photographs Come From Somewhere Else
Two images served live from rounduproofing.com: /images/6-Common-Causes-of-Roof-Leaks---Graves-Brothers1200-×-800.png on your home page, about-us page and roof-repair-leaks page "Graves Brothers" is a different roofing company. /images/Concrete-curb-repair-Dallas-Texas.webp on your home page and about-us page A concrete curb repair photo, on a roofing website. Both return HTTP 200 and are on the live site today.
This is the same fingerprint as the form

A graphic named after another roofing firm and a concrete repair photo, sitting alongside a quote form framed from a concrete contractor's domain. These are not separate problems. They are one builder reusing whatever was to hand across several sites.

The filenames are visible to anyone who looks, and the concrete photo is visible to every customer. Your own job photos are the single cheapest fix on this list — you are a roofer with fifteen years of work behind you and you are showing somebody else's kerb.

Finding 6 — Ten Cities Named, Not One Page For Any Of Them
Pages on the site: 10 (all service or company pages) Pages targeting a specific city: 0 Cities named in your copy: Fort Worth, Plano, Irving, Garland, Frisco, McKinney, Arlington, Denton, Richardson, Mesquite sitemap.xml 404 — not published robots.txt 404 — not published
You have done the hard part and stopped one step short

Naming those ten cities is right. But somebody in Frisco searching "roof repair Frisco" is not going to land on a page about Dallas — and one competitor we checked publishes 258 pages against your ten.

The missing sitemap and robots file are smaller, but they are the two files that tell Google what you have. Both are quick.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Move the form onto your own domain and put the meter on. Your own lead capture, GA4, a Meta pixel and call tracking. First job, and until it is done nothing else can be judged.
  • Find out what your door-knocker is actually worth. Once tracking is live you can see the knock-to-search pattern instead of guessing, and pay him against something real.
  • Build the city pages for the ten suburbs you already name, so Frisco and Plano searches land somewhere that mentions Frisco and Plano.
  • Put your proof on the page — named reviews, a review count, any manufacturer certification you hold, and whether you offer financing.
  • Then get ready for storms. With a pixel running you can be in front of a hit neighbourhood within days, which is the difference between five extra jobs and fifteen.

One man knocking doors is a real channel. It is also a channel with a ceiling and no receipt.

Five to ten more jobs a month needs a second channel that can be measured, and a front door you own.
